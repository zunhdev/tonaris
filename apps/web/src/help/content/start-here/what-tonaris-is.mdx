Tonaris keeps your albums: the ones you love, the ones you mean to hear, and the ones you want to come back to. You sort them the way you think about music. When you want to listen, Tonaris opens the album in Spotify, Apple Music, Qobuz, Tidal or YouTube Music.

## Five ideas

Everything in Tonaris builds on these.

### Albums

Your collection is made of albums, not songs. Each album is in it once, with its title, artist, year and cover. You don't type these: paste a link to the album, and Tonaris fills them in.

### Lists

A list is a set of albums, like a playlist of albums: Late night, To listen, Best of 1974. An album can be in as many lists as you like. It's still one album, so when you fix its year, every list shows the new year.

### Folders

Folders hold lists, and other folders, many levels deep. A folder Moods can hold the lists Late night and Rainy Sundays, and a folder Jazz can hold a folder for each decade.

### Unsorted

An album in no list waits in **Unsorted**. Nothing gets lost: take an album out of its last list, and that's where it goes.

### Your streaming services

Tonaris doesn't play music. Each album keeps a link to each service it's on, and opens there, in the service's web page or its app.

## What else it does

- **Share** a folder or a list with people you invite, to look or to help build it, or with anyone who has its link.
- **Add your own fields** to albums: a rating, the format you own it on, where you bought it, anything.
- **Let an AI assistant help.** Claude, ChatGPT and Codex can look through your collection and change it, and ask you first before deleting anything.
- **Keep everything in step.** Each change is saved as you make it and shows on every device you're signed in on.
- **Take your data with you.** Export the whole collection whenever you like.
- **Use the keyboard.** Almost everything has a key: press <Keys k="?" /> in Tonaris to see them all.

On a phone, you can look through your collection, search it and open albums. Adding and organizing need a computer.
